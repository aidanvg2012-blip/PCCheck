04 / PC BUYER KNOWLEDGE

Know what you are buying

Practical checklists to use before purchasing parts or a used gaming PC.

USED PC BUYING

Before you meet the seller

  • Ask for the exact CPU, GPU, motherboard, RAM, storage, and PSU models.
  • Request a recent video showing the PC booting and running a game or benchmark.
  • Ask whether any parts were repaired, overclocked, or replaced.
  • Confirm what accessories and operating-system license are included.
  • Compare the asking price with several similar systems, not just one listing.
IN-PERSON CHECK

What to inspect

  • Check that the PC starts reliably and recognizes all installed RAM and drives.
  • Look for unexpected shutdowns, crashes, artifacts, or loud unusual noises.
  • Check temperatures during a sustained workload, not only at the desktop.
  • Inspect ports, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, fans, and front-panel connections.
  • Do not pay before you have tested the system and agreed on what is included.
CUSTOM BUILDS

Compatibility checklist

  • Match the CPU socket and confirm the board supports that exact CPU and BIOS version.
  • Match DDR generation; DDR4 and DDR5 are not interchangeable.
  • Check motherboard form factor against the case's supported sizes.
  • Check GPU length, cooler height, radiator support, and slot clearance.
  • Confirm PSU wattage, quality, native connectors, and manufacturer guidance.
  • Check storage interface, M.2 slot sharing, and available SATA ports.
UNDERSTAND THE REPORT

What the labels mean

  • Conflict: entered details contradict a basic compatibility rule.
  • Warning: a risk needs more information or manufacturer verification.
  • Not checked: required details were missing or outside the tool's reference data.
  • Pass: the specific check passed; it does not certify the entire PC.
  • Completeness: how many required listing fields you filled in, not a quality score.
COMMON QUESTIONS

Does PCCheck read marketplace listings automatically?

Not in this version. It recognizes a URL's website and lets you enter the specifications manually. Automatic extraction needs an approved data source or marketplace API and must follow that site's rules.

Does a green result guarantee compatibility?

No. A pass only applies to the check shown. Exact part numbers, BIOS versions, dimensions, connectors, and manufacturer documentation still matter.

Does PCCheck tell me if a used PC is worth the price?

It can compare the asking price with up to three similar listings that you enter. This is a user-supplied comparison, not a live price feed or guaranteed valuation. Compare systems with similar GPUs, CPUs, condition, and included parts.

Are saved reports available on all my devices?

No. Reports are stored in this browser on this device. Cloud accounts and syncing are not connected.

ABOUT PCCHECK

Make better PC decisions.

PCCheck is an independent decision-support tool for people buying used gaming PCs or planning a custom build.

Accuracy before hype

PCCheck uses transparent rule-based checks, not an AI claim or a hidden certification. It can help organize details and surface common issues, but it cannot physically test hardware, verify seller claims, guarantee performance, or replace manufacturer specifications.

Privacy and data

In this prototype, reports stay in your browser's local storage. Account sign-in is currently a beta feature handled by Supabase. Saved reports remain in this browser and are not synced to an account. PCCheck does not provide marketplace account connections or automatic listing scraping. Clearing browser data may remove saved reports. Do not enter passwords, payment details, or other sensitive information.

Independent project

PCCheck is not affiliated with Facebook Marketplace, eBay, Craigslist, OfferUp, AMD, Intel, NVIDIA, or any PC manufacturer. Product and brand names are used only to identify hardware or listing sources.

This is an early product release. Always verify critical details with the manufacturer and inspect a used computer in person before buying.
